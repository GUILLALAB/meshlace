Messages that travel from phone to phone

Meshlace sends encrypted messages over Bluetooth. No internet, no SIM card, no account. When your friend is out of reach, the phones around you carry the message along, without being able to read it.

How it works

There's no server in the middle. Each phone with Meshlace switched on becomes a small part of the network.

  1. 1

    Meet once

    One of you scans the other's code, in person, and you both check a short safety number. That's how you know it's really them.

  2. 2

    Write

    Your message is encrypted on your phone before it goes anywhere. Only your friend can open it.

  3. 3

    It hops

    If your friend is out of range, nearby phones keep the sealed message and pass it on when they meet someone closer.

  4. 4

    Delivered

    You see when it was handed to a phone and when it actually arrived. Nothing is marked delivered unless it was.

Private by the way it's built

Not a promise about what we do with your data. There's simply nowhere for it to go.

  • No servers, no account. No phone number, no email, no sign-up.
  • No internet. The released app doesn't even have permission to go online.
  • End-to-end encrypted. Phones that carry your messages can't read them or tell who they're for.
  • Encrypted on your phone too. Messages are stored encrypted and left out of cloud backups.
  • Quiet notifications. They say "New message", never who or what.
  • No tracking. No ads, no analytics, nothing collected. Read the privacy policy.

Good to know

Bluetooth has limits, and we'd rather you hear them from us.

  • Phones reach each other over a few dozen metres. Messages go further only if other Meshlace phones are around.
  • A message waits up to a day for a path. If none comes, it isn't delivered, and you'll see that.
  • Your phone relays only while Meshlace is on. After a restart, open the app once.
  • Android only for now. iPhones will join later, with tighter limits from Apple in the background.

Questions

Can the phones that carry my messages read them?

No. Messages are sealed on your phone with keys only your contact has. A relaying phone sees a small encrypted package and a random address that changes every few hours.

Does it drain the battery?

It uses Bluetooth Low Energy, which is designed to be light. We're measuring real-world battery use during the beta, and you can switch the mesh off whenever you like.

Who is it for?

Anyone who needs to talk where networks don't reach or can't be trusted: hikes and mountains, festivals, travel without roaming, power cuts, or simply wanting conversations that stay between you.

Is it free?

Yes. There are no ads and no subscription.
